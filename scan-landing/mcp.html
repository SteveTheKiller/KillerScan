What it can do

KillerMCP exposes all nine headless KillerScan commands.

Identify the local networkReturn the active interface, local IPv4 address, subnet, gateway, and DNS server without scanning hosts.
Scan the networkRun bounded quick discovery or full fingerprinting and port checks.
Deep probe one hostInspect one IPv4 host, including ports 1 through 1024.
Look up MAC vendorsUse KillerScan's bundled offline OUI database without contacting a lookup service.
Ping a targetMeasure latency and packet loss with up to 20 ICMP checks.
Trace a routeReturn the network path to an IPv4 address or hostname.
Diagnose a hostCheck DNS, ping, route selection, and selected TCP ports.
Watch hostsSample availability and latency for up to 16 IPv4 addresses.
Run KillerSpeedMeasure download, upload, latency, and jitter through speed.killerscan.net.

Data safety and permissions

KillerScan commands run on your computer. Local network identification does not probe other devices. A quick or full scan does send packets from your computer to the requested target, so review the target and scan type and scan only networks you are authorized to test.

How it works

KillerMCP is a small program that lets your AI assistant use supported Killer apps installed on your computer. You install and connect it once, and it automatically finds the apps and tools available on your computer. When you ask your assistant to inspect a network, KillerMCP checks the address and scan limits, asks KillerScan to run the command locally, waits for it to finish, and gives the results back to your assistant. The scan is performed by the installed KillerScan app, not by this webpage.

Current limits

Targets must be one IPv4 address or a CIDR from /22 through /32. Scans time out after 60 seconds and return no more than 100 discovered devices. Deep probes accept one IPv4 address. Ping sends up to 20 checks, traces return up to 64 hops, diagnostics accept up to 32 ports, and host watches accept up to 16 IPv4 addresses. KillerSpeed can transfer up to 6 GiB of generated test data plus network overhead.
